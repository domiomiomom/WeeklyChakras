Muladhara

Mula (root) + Adhara (support)

Somavara - Monday (Soma - Moon)

🙏 Mudra

Prithvi Mudra

Prithvi (earth)

Position: Ring fingertip to thumb tip, other fingers extended. Do with both hands.

Feeling: Heaviness, solidity, grounding into earth.

🌬️ Pranayama

Nadi Shodhana

Nadi (channel) + Shodhana (purification)

Technique: With the right hand, close the right nostril with the thumb; exhale, then inhale, through the left. Close the left with the ring finger, release the right; exhale, then inhale, through the right. That is one round.

Duration: 5-10 rounds, slow and even. As a beginner, skip any breath retention.

Feel: Left and right settling into balance; the nervous system quieting toward steadiness and support.

🧘 Asanas
Tadasana
Tada (mountain)
Mountain Pose
Feet hip-width, weight even. Press four corners of feet. Lengthen spine, arms by sides.
Uttanasana
Uttana (intense/stretched)
Standing Forward Fold
Fold from hips. Let head hang heavy. Engage legs, soften neck.
Malasana
Mala (garland)
Garland Pose (Deep Squat)
Feet wide, heels grounded if possible. Chest forward. Fold forward.
Vrksasana
Vrksa (tree)
Tree Pose
One foot rooted, other pressing into inner thigh. Hands at heart or overhead. Find stillness.
💡 Learn More: Body & Mind Effects
Body Effects

Prithvi Mudra: Traditionally the earth seal — ring fingertip to thumb — held to steady the earth element. Physiologically, a precise hand shape feeds a strong, steady proprioceptive signal to the brain; the hands carry an outsized share of the sensory cortex, so the gesture quietly anchors attention and lends the practice its grounded, weighted quality.

Nadi Shodhana: Alternate-nostril breathing balances the two sides of the nervous system and lengthens the breath, drawing the body toward parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) tone. That downshift is the physiology of safety — exactly what the root asks for: a body that feels supported and unhurried.

Grounding Asanas: Pressing the four corners of the feet into the earth, Tadasana and Vrksasana build proprioceptive awareness of the base, and Malasana releases tension in the deep hip stabilisers. Forward folds quiet the nervous system, easing it toward parasympathetic tone.

Mind & Spirit Effects

Root Chakra Shift: Muladhara correlates with the feeling of safety and belonging. Grounding practices signal the nervous system that you are supported. Stability in the body translates to stability in the mind—a foundation for all higher practices.

What You Should Feel: Heaviness in the legs, a sense of being held by the earth, reduced anxiety, increased presence. Students often report feeling "anchored" or more able to handle stress.

Svadhisthana

Sva (own) + Adhisthana (dwelling place)

Mangalavara - Tuesday (Mangala - Mars)

🙏 Mudra

Varuna Mudra

Varuna (water)

Position: Little fingertip to thumb tip, other fingers extended. Both hands.

Feeling: Flow, fluidity, emotional ease.

🌬️ Pranayama

Bhramari

Bhramari (bhramara — bee)

Technique: Inhale fully through the nose. Exhale with the lips gently closed, making a soft, steady humming sound — like a bee. Optionally rest the index fingers lightly on the ear cartilage.

Duration: 5-8 rounds, building gradually. Keep the hum low and even.

Deepening: Chandra Bhedana (chandra — moon, bhedana — piercing). Left-nostril-only breathing, which activates Ida nadi — the cooling, lunar channel that matches the water element.

Feel: Vibration spreading through the skull and chest; a fluid, soothing settling of the nervous system.

🧘 Asanas
Baddha Konasana
Baddha (bound) + Kona (angle)
Bound Angle Pose
Soles of feet together. Press knees down gently. Fold forward slightly or stay upright.
Eka Pada Rajakapotasana
Eka (one) + Pada (foot) + Raja (king) + Kapota (pigeon)
Pigeon Pose
One leg forward bent, hip flexion. Back leg extended. Fold forward as available.
Anjaneyasana
Anjaneya (a name of Hanuman, son of Anjana)
Low Lunge (Crescent)
Back knee down. Front foot forward. Square hips. Reach arms up or rest on front thigh.
💡 Learn More: Body & Mind Effects
Body Effects

Varuna Mudra: Traditionally the water seal — little fingertip to thumb — held to invite the fluid, flowing quality of the water element. Like every mudra, its measurable effect is attentional: the precise hand shape gives the mind a steady proprioceptive anchor, supporting a soft, unforced focus.

Bhramari: The humming creates a vibration that carries through the bones of the skull and down into the chest. The long, gentle exhale stimulates the vagus nerve and shifts the body toward parasympathetic (rest-and-digest) dominance. Of all the pranayamas it most closely echoes seed-mantra chanting — sound felt as a settling, fluid resonance.

Hip Openers: Baddha Konasana and Pigeon open the deep, often-guarded tissues of the hip joint. Intense, unfamiliar sensation here can prompt autonomic shifts and, sometimes, emotional release — not because the tissue "stores" memory, but because the nervous system reads the body closely (interoception), and a vulnerable area letting go can surface feeling. If emotion arises, it is welcome.

Mind & Spirit Effects

Sacral Chakra Shift: Svadhisthana governs sexuality, creativity, and emotional fluidity. Opening this chakra allows blocked emotions to flow. Students often experience creative insights or emotional releases in hip openers.

What You Should Feel: Pleasure, fluidity, ease in the hips and belly. Emotional releases are normal. A sense of creative possibility and sensory aliveness.

Manipura

Mani (gem) + Pura (city)

Budhavara - Wednesday (Budha - Mercury)

🙏 Mudra

Agni Mudra

Agni (fire)

Position: Ring finger folded into the palm, thumb pressing it down. Other fingers extended. Both hands.

Feeling: Heat, power, willpower activation.

🌬️ Pranayama

Kapalabhati

Kapala (skull) + Bhati (shining)

Technique: Passive inhale, active exhale through nose (via abdomen contraction). Rapid cycles.

Duration: 30 seconds to 1 minute. Rest between rounds.

Feel: Cleansing, energizing, mental clarity.

🧘 Asanas
Navasana
Nava (boat)
Boat Pose
Sit, lift chest. Knees bent initially, shins parallel to ground. Lean back slightly.
Phalakasana
Phalaka (plank/board)
Plank Pose
Shoulders over wrists. Body in line. Core engaged. Press through palms.
Chaturanga Dandasana
Chatur (four) + Anga (limb) + Danda (staff)
Four-Limbed Staff Pose
Lower from plank. Elbows hug ribs. Shoulders over wrists. Lower as far as comfortable.
Parivrtta Trikonasana
Parivrtta (revolved) + Trikona (triangle)
Revolved Triangle Pose
Forward fold with feet hip-width. Place opposite hand to foot. Rotate chest open.
💡 Learn More: Body & Mind Effects
Body Effects

Agni Mudra: Traditionally the fire seal: the ring finger carries the earth element, so folding it down and pinning it with the thumb is said to reduce earth and let fire rise — kindling agni. Its reliable, physical effect is the one shared by all mudras — a precise hand shape that anchors attention — here paired with the warm, willful focus of the solar-plexus practice.

Kapalabhati: Rapid abdominal contractions massage the liver, pancreas, and spleen. Cleanses the nasal passages. The aggressive exhale stimulates the sympathetic nervous system—energizing and clarifying the mind. Boosts digestion and metabolism.

Core Engagement: Navasana, Plank, and Chaturanga build agni through muscular activation. The heat and effort generate digestive and transformative fire. These poses also strengthen the manipura's physical seat in the navel plexus.

Mind & Spirit Effects

Power Center Activation: Manipura is the seat of willpower, confidence, and personal power. These practices ignite your ability to transform, act, and manifest intention. Students report increased motivation and clarity of purpose.

What You Should Feel: Heat, aliveness, confidence, mental clarity. A sense of "I can." Energy and focus returning to the center. Warmth radiating from the belly.

Anahata

An (not) + Ahata (struck)

Brihaspativara - Thursday (Brihaspati - Jupiter)

🙏 Mudra

Padma Mudra

Padma (lotus)

Position: Heels of the hands together at the heart, thumbs and little fingers touching; let the three middle fingers bloom open like lotus petals.

Feeling: Expansion, openness, compassion — the heart unfolding.

🌬️ Pranayama

Sama Vritti

Sama (equal) + Vritti (fluctuation / movement)

Technique: Equal count inhale, equal count exhale (e.g., 4 counts each). No pause.

Duration: 5-10 minutes. Calming and centering.

Feel: Balance, integration, heart-centered calm.

🧘 Asanas
Bhujangasana
Bhujanga (serpent/cobra)
Cobra Pose
Palms under shoulders. Chest forward and up. Shoulders back. Keep elbows close to ribs.
Ustrasana
Ustra (camel)
Camel Pose
Knees hip-width. Hands to heels or blocks. Chest lifts, head may drop back gently.
Matsyasana
Matsya (fish)
Fish Pose
Lie back on forearms. Chest opens upward. Head may rest on crown of head.
💡 Learn More: Body & Mind Effects
Body Effects

Padma Mudra: The lotus opening — a traditional gesture of the heart unfolding, held at the sternum. Like every mudra, its dependable effect is attentional: the precise shape anchors awareness at the heart centre, exactly where the practice is directed.

Sama Vritti: Equal-ratio breathing steadies the rhythm of the breath, and a steady breath steadies the heart — slow, even breathing improves heart-rate variability and vagal tone, the physiological signature of a calm, coherent system. This is the breath of integration.

Chest Openers: Bhujangasana, Ustrasana, and Matsyasana stretch the pectoral muscles and fascia of the front body, opening the anterior chest where we habitually collapse. As the chest opens and the breath deepens, the nervous system reads it as safety and softens.

Mind & Spirit Effects

Heart Center Awakening: Anahata is the bridge between the lower (material) and upper (spiritual) chakras. It governs love, compassion, and integration of opposites. Students often experience emotional softening and an increase in capacity for love—both for others and self.

What You Should Feel: Expansion in the chest, lightness, warmth in the heart area. Compassion. A felt sense of wholeness. Emotions may surface—grief, joy, tenderness. This is healing.

Vishuddha

Vishuddhi (purification)

Shukravara - Friday (Shukra - Venus)

🙏 Mudra

Vishuddha Mudra

Vishuddha (purification)

Position: All fingers interlaced, thumbs upright and touching. Held at throat or chest.

Feeling: Clarity, expression, truth.

🌬️ Pranayama

Ujjayi

Ujjayi (victorious — ud, expanding + ji, to conquer)

Technique: Slight constriction at throat (like fogging a mirror). Audible ocean-like sound. Equal inhale and exhale. Add Jalandhara Bandha: slight chin tuck, back of neck lengthened.

Duration: 5-10 minutes.

Feel: Grounded expression, energy contained and directed.

🧘 Asanas
Simhasana
Simha (lion)
Lion Pose
Knees wide, sit on heels. Exhale with open mouth, extend tongue, roar or breathe audibly.
Matsyasana
Matsya (fish)
Fish Pose
Lie back, lift the chest, rest the crown of the head lightly on the floor. The front of the throat opens and lengthens — a direct counter to forward folds.
Parivrtta Sukhasana
Parivrtta (revolved) + Sukha (easy)
Revolved Easy Pose (Gentle Twist)
Seated cross-legged. Twist toward one side. Hand to opposite knee. Lengthen spine.
💡 Learn More: Body & Mind Effects
Body Effects

Vishuddha Mudra: Interlaced fingers with upright thumbs — a traditional gesture for the integration of the five elements at the throat. As with every mudra, its measurable role is attentional: the precise hand shape anchors awareness at the throat centre. A hand position cannot reach or drive the thyroid — but steady focus here, paired with the breath below, is what the practice relies on.

Ujjayi with Jalandhara Bandha: The throat lock (slight chin tuck) stimulates the vagus nerve and the carotid sinus. This reflex naturally slows the heart and deepens parasympathetic tone. The constricted throat causes air to move more slowly through the vocal cords, creating the distinctive ocean sound—which itself has a meditative, soothing effect.

Throat-Focused Asanas: Simhasana stretches the muscles and fascia of the throat directly, and the lion's roar releases tension held in the throat and jaw — places where we habitually hold back the voice. Matsyasana opens and lengthens the front of the throat, while the gentle twist of Parivrtta Sukhasana frees the neck.

Mind & Spirit Effects

Voice & Truth: Vishuddha is the center of authentic expression and creative truth. When blocked, we can't speak our truth. These practices clear blockages and strengthen the capacity to communicate with clarity and integrity.

What You Should Feel: Clarity of thought. Ease in expressing yourself. A sense that your words matter. Freedom in the throat. Students often report feeling more creative and articulate after throat chakra work.

Ajna

Ajna (command)

Shanivara - Saturday (Shani - Saturn)

🙏 Mudra

Hakini Mudra

Hakini (the deity of the Ajna chakra)

Position: Bring the fingertips and thumb tips of both hands to touch, lightly — each fingertip meeting its mirror. Hold the hands before the chest or brow.

Feeling: Inner sight, intuition, stillness — the two sides drawn together.

🌬️ Pranayama

Anuloma Viloma

Anuloma (with the grain) + Viloma (against the grain)

Technique: Alternate-nostril breathing without retention — exhale and inhale through one nostril, then the other, using the right hand to close each in turn. Slow, even, unhurried.

Duration: 5-10 rounds. Balances the left and right channels and settles the mind for focus.

Focus practice: Trataka (trataka — steady gazing). Not a breath but a concentration practice — one of the six shatkarmas. Gaze softly at a candle flame or single point for 2-5 minutes; it trains undistracted attention, the classical preparation for Ajna.

Feel: Clarity, intuition, mental quietude.

🧘 Asanas
Paschimottanasana
Paschima (west / back of the body) + Uttana (intense stretch)
Seated Forward Fold
Legs extended. Fold from hips. Let head hang. Breathe into the fold.
Balasana
Bala (child)
Child's Pose
Knees wide, big toes touching. Sink hips to heels. Forehead down. Arms forward or alongside.
Sukhasana
Sukha (easy)
Easy Pose (Simple Cross-Legged)
Seated cross-legged. Sit upright. Hands on knees. Ground sitting bones.
💡 Learn More: Body & Mind Effects
Body Effects

Hakini Mudra: The fingertips of both hands meeting their mirror — a traditional gesture said to "join" the two sides in attention. Its dependable effect is the one shared by all mudras: a precise, symmetrical hand shape that anchors awareness, here gathered at the brow where the practice is directed.

Trataka: Trataka is not a breathing practice but a concentration technique — one of the six shatkarmas. Holding a soft, steady gaze on a single point trains the mind to rest without distraction; that sustained attention, not any change to the eye itself, is the real benefit, and the classical preparation for meditation.

Anuloma Viloma: Alternate-nostril breathing — here without retention — is traditionally said to balance the brain's two hemispheres, the left nostril linked to the right hemisphere and the right nostril to the left. Some research on unilateral nostril breathing shows partial autonomic and EEG effects; the clean crossover is best held as a traditional map. Either way, the practice reliably settles and clarifies the mind.

Inward-Gazing Asanas: Forward folds and child's pose naturally turn the gaze and attention inward. The forehead resting toward the earth is a symbolic surrender of the thinking mind to quiet awareness.

Mind & Spirit Effects

Third Eye Opening: Ajna is the center of intuition, inner vision, and the command center of consciousness. When open, you access intuitive knowing—wisdom that transcends logical thought. You see through illusions and understand the deeper patterns of existence.

What You Should Feel: Mental clarity and calmness. Intuitive insights arising. A sense of "seeing" beyond the physical. Some students report visual phenomena during practice—this is normal. A quieting of the mind's chatter. Deep peace.

Sahasrara

Sahasra (thousand) + Ara (petal)

Ravivara - Sunday (Ravi - Sun)

🙏 Mudra

Sahasrara Mudra

Sahasrara (thousand-petaled)

Position: All fingers interlaced, index fingers extended upward touching. Held at crown or above head.

Feeling: Expansion, unity, transcendence.

🌬️ Pranayama

Dirga Pranayama

Dirga (long/full)

Technique: Inhale filling belly → ribcage → chest. Exhale in reverse. Integrates entire breath body.

Alternative: Silent meditation. Sit. Release technique. Witness breath naturally. 10-20 minutes.

Feel: Wholeness, peace, dissolution of boundaries.

🧘 Asanas
Sirsasana
Sirsa (head)
Headstand
Crown of head on ground, forearms parallel, elbows shoulder-width. Legs up. Work toward this gradually.
Adho Mukha Svanasana
Adho (downward) + Mukha (face) + Svana (dog)
Downward-Facing Dog
Hands shoulder-width, feet hip-width. Hips high, head between arms. Press firmly through palms.
Padmasana
Padma (lotus)
Lotus Pose
Seated, fold one leg then the other into lotus (or half-lotus). Spine tall. Hands in mudra.
💡 Learn More: Body & Mind Effects
Body Effects

Sahasrara Mudra: Interlaced fingers with the index fingers raised — a traditional gesture for the unity of all elements at the crown. Like every mudra, what it reliably does is gather attention: a still, deliberate hand shape that holds awareness at the top of the head, where the practice dissolves into openness.

Dirga Pranayama: The three-part breath fills the belly, ribs and chest in turn, using the full capacity of the lungs. Slow and complete, it improves gas exchange and strongly favours vagal, parasympathetic tone — a whole-body settling that suits the crown's theme of integration.

Crown Asanas: Inversions like Headstand and Downward Dog reverse the body's usual relationship to gravity — draining venous and lymphatic fluid the other way and loading the baroreceptors, while the shift in perspective is felt as much as it is physical. This literal turning-upside-down mirrors the inward turn of consciousness. Lotus Pose is the traditional seat of meditation, holding the spine tall and still.

Mind & Spirit Effects

Crown Chakra Awakening: Sahasrara is not a chakra in the traditional sense—it is the seat of pure consciousness, unity, and enlightenment. When awakened, you experience yourself as boundless, as part of all existence. The ego dissolves. You realize there is no separation between self and other, between individual and universal consciousness.

What You Should Feel: Profound peace and stillness. A sense of vastness and spaciousness. Unity with all beings. The dissolution of the sense of separate self. Bliss (ananda). Some students experience this as overwhelming, and that is normal—the nervous system is learning to hold a new frequency. Sahasrara work is the ultimate integration of all six lower chakras into the infinite.
